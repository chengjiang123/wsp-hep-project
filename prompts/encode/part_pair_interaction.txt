You are a particle physicist who knows jet substructure and transformers. Write, by hand and without any data, the
pair-interaction term of a Particle Transformer for jet tagging. You will not see any trained network or labelled jet.

The task. Jets are anti-kT R = 0.8 jets with pT between 550 and 650 GeV, |eta| < 2, from 14 TeV pp collisions with a
Delphes detector simulation. Signal is hadronically decaying top quarks (t -> b W -> b q q'), background is QCD
light-quark and gluon jets. Each jet is a set of up to 128 particles (pT, eta, phi, E).

The architecture. A transformer with 8 attention heads per layer acts on the particles. In every layer, before the
softmax over j, each head h adds a bias U_h(i, j) to the attention logit of particle i attending to particle j:
    attention_h(i, j) = softmax_j( q_i . k_j / sqrt(d) + U_h(i, j) )
The same eight biases are used in all eight layers. Typical logits q.k/sqrt(d) are of order 1 to 5, so a bias of a few
units already steers a head strongly. Padded particles are masked.
U_h(i, j) depends only on four features of the pair (natural logarithms, momenta in GeV):
    lnkT  = ln(min(pT_i, pT_j) * DeltaR_ij), pT in GeV. On real pairs: mean -0.548, sd 1.274, quantiles 1%: -3.11, 5%: -2.256, 25%: -1.25, 50%: -0.572, 75%: 0.17, 95%: 1.358, 99%: 2.209
    lnz   = ln(min(pT_i, pT_j) / (pT_i + pT_j)). On real pairs: mean -1.865, sd 1.039, quantiles 1%: -5.151, 5%: -3.985, 25%: -2.398, 50%: -1.57, 75%: -1.055, 95%: -0.757, 99%: -0.706
    lnDR  = ln(DeltaR_ij), DeltaR in the rapidity-azimuth plane. On real pairs: mean -1.046, sd 1.059, quantiles 1%: -3.873, 5%: -2.679, 25%: -1.433, 50%: -0.825, 75%: -0.423, 95%: -0.033, 99%: 0.191
    lnm2  = ln(m_ij^2), invariant mass squared of the pair in GeV^2. On real pairs: mean 0.524, sd 2.248, quantiles 1%: -4.723, 5%: -2.941, 25%: -0.879, 50%: 0.509, 75%: 1.971, 95%: 4.114, 99%: 5.552
Each head is
    U_h = GELU( p_h(lnkT, lnz, lnDR, lnm2) )
where p_h is a polynomial of degree at most 3 that you write, in the raw features above (not standardised).
GELU(z) is about z for large positive z and about 0 for large negative z, with a minimum of -0.17, so a head can only
raise the attention between some pairs relative to the rest. Values far outside the ranges above are clipped to about
three standard deviations before the polynomial is applied.

Goal: biases that make the attention heads useful for telling top jets from QCD jets, when the rest of the transformer
is trained on only about 1000 labelled jets. Think about which pairs of particles each head should attend between, and
give the eight heads different jobs.

Answer with a short paragraph of physics reasoning for each head, then ONE JSON object in a ```json block:
{"note": "<one line>", "heads": [{"job": "<what this head looks for>", "terms": {"1": c0, "lnDR": c1, "lnm2*lnDR": c2, "lnkT^2": c3, ...}}, ... 8 heads]}
Term names are products of lnkT, lnz, lnDR, lnm2 joined by "*", with powers as ^2 or ^3, total degree at most 3.
"1" is the constant. Leave out terms you do not need.
